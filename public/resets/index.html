How to read this

When (UTC) is the value exactly as the official source states it. Where a publisher announces a day without a time — Riot's patch schedule does this — the row shows the date alone, because a time would be ours rather than theirs.

Your time is that same instant in the time zone your device is set to. It is only filled in where the source gave an exact time; a date announced without one is the same date almost everywhere, and converting it would invent a moment nobody published.

How often is filled in two ways, and left empty when neither applies. GTA Online's weekly reset is a rule Rockstar publishes, so it is quoted as a rule. The rest is read from each game's own history: where the published dates are evenly spaced, the spacing is reported as "about every" that long. Counter-Strike has no entry because Valve ships updates when they are ready, and pretending otherwise would be inventing a rhythm.

Status says how fresh the value is. Live means the source answered on the most recent check. Stale means it did not, and the last verified value is being shown rather than an error. No date means the publisher has not announced one — we show that instead of guessing.

A note on daylight saving

UTC does not shift, but your clock does. GTA Online resets at 10:00 UTC every week of the year; in New York that is 6:00 AM through the summer and 5:00 AM once the clocks go back. Nothing about the reset moved — the offset between you and UTC did. The "Your time" column is computed by your own browser, so it already accounts for this on the day it changes.
